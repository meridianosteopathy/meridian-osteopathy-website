# IPP3A compliance — operational playbook

**Status:** active from 1 May 2026 · **Owner:** Nina Hu (Privacy Officer)

This is the clinic-side companion to the website changes. It exists because
**a privacy policy on its own is not enough** under IPP3A — the law requires us
to *actively notify* patients whenever we collect their health information
indirectly (i.e. from someone other than the patient). Most of that happens at
reception, in Cliniko, and in conversation — not on the website.

> Source: Osteopaths New Zealand, *IPP3A and Indirect Collection of Health
> Information* (30 April 2026). Authoritative guidance: Office of the Privacy
> Commissioner — <https://privacy.org.nz/resources-and-learning/a-z-topics/ipp3a/>.
> This playbook is operational guidance, not legal advice; get a solicitor to
> review before relying on it for significant policy decisions.

---

## The one rule for the whole team

> **If the information did not come directly from the patient, notify the patient.**

Examples of *indirect* collection (notify): a referral or report from another
provider, ACC claim/injury information, notes from a co-managing provider,
information a family member gives us about the patient.

Not indirect (no IPP3A notice needed): the patient fills in our own intake form,
tells us their history in the room, or brings their own paperwork in. That is
*direct* collection.

---

## The six things every notice must cover

1. **What** we've collected (and that it came from someone else)
2. **Why** — the purpose (to arrange and provide care)
3. **Who** else sees it (clinical team; ACC if there's an active claim)
4. **Who we are** — clinic name, address, contact details
5. **Any legal basis** — e.g. ACC collection under the Accident Compensation Act 2001
6. **Their rights** — how to access or correct the information

A notice that misses #4 or #5 is not compliant. Notification is a **one-time**
requirement per piece of information — no need to repeat it every visit.

---

## What the website already handles (so you don't have to)

- **Privacy Policy** (`/privacy/`) — now has a dedicated *section 3* covering all
  six elements for indirect collection.
- **Website referral form** — when someone refers a patient through
  `meridianosteopathy.co.nz/referral`, the patient is **emailed an IPP3A notice
  automatically**. You do **not** need to re-notify that patient — it's already
  done. (The clinician can still mention it in passing at the first visit, but
  isn't required to.)

Everything below is for referrals/reports/ACC information that arrive **any other
way** (HealthLink, email, fax, post, phone, in person).

## Operational checklist

### 1. Cliniko

- [ ] **Add a layered privacy line** to the *appointment confirmation* email/SMS
      and the *new-patient welcome* template (see template B below).
- [ ] **Create a reusable communication template** "We've received your
      referral/report" (template A) so reception can send it in two clicks when
      something arrives from another provider or ACC.
- [ ] **Add the privacy paragraph** (template C) to the Cliniko **online intake
      form**. (A patient filling our own form is *direct* collection, so IPP3A
      doesn't strictly apply — but it's the natural place to disclose that we
      sometimes receive information about them from others.)
- [ ] **Record that notice was given** — a quick note or flag on the patient
      file. If you ever rely on an exception (below), write down why.

### 2. Reception / daily workflow

- [ ] Apply the one rule above to every incoming document.
- [ ] When a referral, report, or ACC detail lands and the patient hasn't already
      been notified (e.g. via the website) → send template A, **or** make sure the
      clinician notifies verbally at the first appointment (script D).
- [ ] Avoid double-notifying: if the website already emailed them, or the
      referrer told them we'd receive it, you don't need to send template A.

### 3. Staff awareness / training

- [ ] Brief everyone involved in intake (currently you, plus any future admin) on
      the one rule and the six elements.
- [ ] Keep a dated file note that the briefing happened (evidence of training).

### 4. Regular referrers

- [ ] For any practice that refers to us often, agree they name **"Meridian
      Osteopathy"** specifically in *their own* privacy notice. Then their patients
      already know on arrival and we don't have to duplicate the notice.
- [ ] Keep written evidence of that arrangement (an email or a clause is enough).
      Note: **we remain responsible** if the referrer fails to notify, so this
      only helps where we trust the partner.

### 5. Review third-party systems

- [ ] **ACC workflow** — confirm our notice (or template A) covers ACC-sourced
      information and cites the Accident Compensation Act 2001.
- [ ] **Any online booking/referral platform** — confirm it either notifies the
      patient on our behalf (naming Meridian + all six elements) or that our own
      process fills the gap.

### 6. Insurance

- [ ] Review professional-indemnity wording (QBE) on privacy cover. Insurance is
      **not** a substitute for compliance — fix the systems first.

## Copy-paste templates

> House style: never imply a patient needs a referral to see us, and avoid the
> term "GP" — say "your doctor or another health provider".

### Template A — "We've received your referral/report" (Cliniko email)

```
Subject: We've received your referral — Meridian Osteopathy

Hi [First name],

We're letting you know that Meridian Osteopathy has received [a referral /
a report / your injury information] about you from [your doctor / the radiology
clinic / ACC / another provider]. Under the Privacy Act 2020 we tell you when we
receive your health information from someone other than you.

We'll use it to arrange and provide your care. It's seen only by our clinical
team, and by ACC if you have an active claim with us (which may be authorised
under the Accident Compensation Act 2001).

You can ask to see this information, or have it corrected, at any time — just
contact our Privacy Officer, Nina Hu, at info@meridianosteopathy.co.nz or
02108655151. Our full Privacy Policy is at meridianosteopathy.co.nz/privacy.

Ngā mihi,
The team at Meridian Osteopathy
21 Coppell Place, Hillmorton, Christchurch 8025
```

### Template B — layered line for appointment-confirmation / welcome templates

```
Privacy: if we receive information about you from another provider or ACC, we'll
let you know. See how we handle your information at meridianosteopathy.co.nz/privacy.
```

### Template C — paragraph for the Cliniko online intake form

```
How we use your information

We use the details you give us to arrange and deliver your care. Sometimes we
also receive information about you from others — for example a referral from your
doctor, a report from another provider, or your ACC claim details — and when that
happens we'll let you know. You can ask to see or correct your information at any
time. Read our full Privacy Policy at meridianosteopathy.co.nz/privacy.
```

### Script D — verbal notice at the first appointment

Sole practitioner (or clinician), at the start of the first consult:

> "We received a referral/report about you from [your doctor / the radiology
> clinic / another provider] before today. We'll use it as part of your
> assessment, and you're welcome to see it or ask us to correct anything."

Where reception has already emailed the patient, the clinician just follows
through:

> "You may have been emailed to say we received your referral notes — I've had a
> look and we'll use them as part of today's assessment."

Keep it brief, plain, and unalarming. You don't need to read from a script.

## When you don't need to notify (use narrowly, and write down why)

- The patient has **already been told** — e.g. by the referrer, or by our website
  email — and you have a reasonable basis to believe so.
- Information is **publicly available**.
- Notifying is **not reasonably practicable** (we hold no contact details and it's
  not reasonable to collect them just to notify). Cost/inconvenience alone is *not*
  a good enough reason.
- Information is **de-identified / used only in aggregate**.

In routine osteopathic practice these rarely apply. Don't assume an exception —
document your reasoning if you rely on one.

## Quick reference: who notifies, when

| Information arrives via…            | Who notifies            | How                         |
| ----------------------------------- | ----------------------- | --------------------------- |
| Website referral form               | **Automated** (website) | Email to patient on submit  |
| Email / HealthLink / fax / post     | Reception               | Template A, or script D     |
| ACC claim / injury detail           | Reception / clinician   | Template A (cite ACC Act)   |
| Family/caregiver gives info in room | Clinician               | Script D                    |
| Patient fills our own intake form   | — (direct collection)   | No IPP3A notice required    |
