## What acupuncture offers during pregnancy

Pregnancy is one of the periods where people are most cautious about what they put into their bodies — and one of the periods where they most need something that helps them sleep, calms nausea, and takes the edge off the physical intensity of it all. Acupuncture is one of the few options that ticks both boxes: it's drug-free, well-tolerated, and has decades of research and clinical use in pregnancy behind it.

At Meridian we adapt our approach for each trimester. In early pregnancy the focus is usually nausea, fatigue and anxiety. In the middle trimester we often see people for sleep, digestion and general tension. Later on, the focus shifts toward back and pelvic discomfort, swelling, and — from around 36 weeks — labour preparation. The needle count is typically small (six to twelve), the depth is shallow, and the atmosphere in the room is deliberately quiet.

## Who this is for

People most often book pregnancy acupuncture for:

- **Nausea and morning sickness**, including hyperemesis-adjacent symptoms in early pregnancy
- **Disturbed sleep** — trouble falling asleep, waking at 3am, or discomfort turning over
- **Anxiety, low mood, or "wound up" feelings** — including the emotional side of pregnancy after loss or IVF
- **Headaches and migraines**, particularly when medications you'd normally reach for are off-limits
- **Reflux, constipation, or a very tight diaphragm** as the baby grows upward
- **General fatigue** and a sense of your reserves being depleted
- **Labour preparation** from around 36 weeks onward (a separate protocol — see our labour-preparation post)

If your midwife or usual doctor is happy for you to have acupuncture, we're happy to see you.

## What a session looks like

Your first visit takes about 60 minutes. We talk through your history — how you're feeling, how the pregnancy is going, any medications you're taking, previous experience with acupuncture — and then you'll settle onto the treatment table in whatever position is comfortable. From about 20 weeks that's usually side-lying with pillow support.

Point selection depends on what we're treating: nausea and morning sickness draw on a well-established set of upper-limb and wrist points; sleep and anxiety add points on the head, ears and forearms; general fatigue often benefits from points on the hands and lower legs. Once the needles are in, you rest quietly for 20–30 minutes while they do their work. Many people fall asleep — that's fine.

Between sessions we may suggest self-acupressure on one or two easy-to-find points (particularly for nausea), so you have something to reach for at 3am without waiting for the next appointment.

## Working alongside your maternity team

Acupuncture doesn't replace anything your midwife, obstetrician or usual doctor is doing. It sits alongside — a complementary option that many people find genuinely helpful for the symptoms modern medicine has fewer good tools for, particularly in early pregnancy when most medications are avoided. If there's ever anything about your pregnancy that needs urgent attention, we'll say so and encourage you to raise it with them straight away.
