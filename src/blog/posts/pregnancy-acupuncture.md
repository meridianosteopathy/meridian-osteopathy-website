## What acupuncture offers during pregnancy

During pregnancy, many people are careful about what they put into their bodies, yet it's also when they most need help with sleep, nausea and the physical demands of it all. Acupuncture offers both: it's drug-free, well tolerated, and has decades of research and clinical use in pregnancy behind it.

At Meridian, we adapt treatment to each stage of pregnancy. Early on, the focus is usually nausea, tiredness and anxiety. In the middle months, it's often sleep, digestion and tension. Later, it shifts to back and pelvic pain, swelling and, from around 36 weeks, labour preparation. We use only a few needles (six to twelve), placed shallowly, in a quiet, calm room.

## Who this is for

People most often book pregnancy acupuncture for:

- **Nausea and morning sickness**, including stronger sickness in early pregnancy
- **Poor sleep**: trouble falling asleep, waking at 3am, or discomfort turning over
- **Anxiety, low mood or feeling "wound up"**, including pregnancy after loss or IVF
- **Headaches and migraines**, especially when your usual medicines are off-limits
- **Reflux, constipation or a tight diaphragm** as your baby grows upward
- **Tiredness** and feeling run down
- **Labour preparation** from around 36 weeks (see our labour preparation post)

If your midwife or usual doctor is happy for you to have acupuncture, we're happy to see you.

## What a session looks like

Your first visit takes 40–60 minutes. We'll talk about how you're feeling, how your pregnancy is going, any medicines you take, and whether you've had acupuncture before. Then you'll settle onto the treatment table in whatever position is comfortable. From about 20 weeks, that's usually on your side with pillows.

The points we use depend on what we're treating. For nausea, we use well-known points on the arms and wrists. For sleep and anxiety, we add points on the head, ears and forearms. For tiredness, we often use points on the hands and lower legs. Once the needles are in, you rest quietly for 20–30 minutes. Many people fall asleep, and that's fine.

Between sessions, we may show you one or two easy acupressure points to press yourself (especially for nausea), so you have something to try at 3am without waiting for your next appointment.

## Working alongside your maternity team

Acupuncture doesn't replace anything your midwife, obstetrician or usual doctor is doing. It works alongside their care, and many people find it helps with symptoms that have few other good options, especially in early pregnancy when most medicines are avoided. If anything about your pregnancy needs urgent attention, we'll tell you and encourage you to contact them straight away.
