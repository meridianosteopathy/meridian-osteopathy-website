## What sciatica is

Sciatica is pain that travels along the sciatic nerve, the longest nerve in the body. It runs from your lower back, through your buttock, and down the back of your leg towards your foot. The pain can be a dull ache, or sharp, burning or electric. It often comes with pins and needles, numbness or weakness in the leg.

Sciatica is a symptom rather than a diagnosis. The most common causes are a disc bulge in the lower back pressing on a nerve root, narrowing of the spinal canal (stenosis), arthritic changes in the small joints of the lower back, or a tight piriformis muscle in the buttock irritating the nerve. Part of a first visit is working out which of these is most likely, because that shapes the treatment.

## How long does sciatica last?

Many milder episodes settle within 4–6 weeks with gentle movement, simple pain relief and time. Getting assessed early often shortens recovery, lowers the chance of it coming back, and helps you stay active while it settles.

It's worth booking in if:

- The pain has lasted more than a week, or is making sitting, driving or sleep difficult
- Painkillers and rest aren't shifting it within 7–10 days
- It started after a specific lift, fall or twist (so it can be assessed for ACC cover)
- It keeps coming back, or has flared several times in the past year
- You have ongoing pins and needles or mild weakness, but none of the urgent signs below

## When to get help urgently

Some symptoms need a doctor straight away, not an osteopath. Go to your nearest emergency department, or call your doctor urgently, if you have:

- Loss of bladder or bowel control
- Numbness around your groin or inner thighs
- Leg weakness that is getting worse quickly
- Sciatica after a significant fall or accident

These can be signs of cauda equina syndrome, a rare but serious condition that needs immediate specialist care.

## Sciatica in pregnancy

Sciatica-like pain down one leg is common in pregnancy, as your posture changes and the ligaments around your pelvis soften. Gentle osteopathy is safe in every trimester. From around 20 weeks, we treat you lying on your side or propped up with pillows, and nothing is forceful. Kaylee leads our pregnancy osteopathy. You can read more about [pregnancy back and pelvic pain](/conditions/pregnancy-back-pain/) or [osteopathy through pregnancy](/blog/pregnancy-osteopathy/).

Pregnancy-related sciatica usually isn't covered by ACC, because it comes on gradually rather than from an accident. If your pain started after a specific event, mention it when you book.

## What happens at your first visit

Your first appointment includes:

- **A detailed history**: when the pain started, where it goes, what helps and what makes it worse
- **A nerve check**: reflexes, sensation and strength in your legs and feet
- **Movement testing** of your lower back, hips and pelvis, to find what is loading the nerve
- **A plain-language explanation** of what's most likely going on, and a realistic timeline for recovery
- **Hands-on treatment**: gentle joint articulation, soft-tissue release around the buttock, and nerve gliding
- **Two or three exercises** to do at home without flaring the nerve
- **ACC cover** set up if an accident started it, plus a clear plan for review

Where it helps, acupuncture (dry needling) can be added to your treatment. It can be useful for stubborn muscle spasm in the buttock. Read more on our [sciatica treatment page](/conditions/sciatica/).
