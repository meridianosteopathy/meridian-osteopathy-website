## Why pregnancy asks so much of your body

Pregnancy changes the way you stand, move and sleep — often faster than the muscles and joints supporting you can adapt. As the baby grows, your centre of gravity shifts forward, the lower back curve deepens, and the pelvic ligaments soften under the influence of relaxin. Add in longer periods of sitting, a full bladder that changes gait, and disrupted sleep, and it's not surprising that lower-back, pelvic-girdle and hip discomfort are among the most common physical complaints in pregnancy.

Gentle osteopathy is one of the safest and best-tolerated ways to keep your body moving well through those changes. The aim isn't to "fix" a pregnancy — your body is doing exactly what it should — but to help the joints, muscles and connective tissues around your spine, pelvis and rib cage share the load more evenly, so you can sleep, walk and get on with your day with less pain.

## Who this is for

Pregnancy osteopathy at Meridian is designed for anyone experiencing:

- Lower-back pain, especially when it worsens with standing or turning in bed
- Pelvic-girdle pain — pain across the pubic symphysis or sacroiliac joints, sometimes described as "stabbing" when climbing stairs or rolling over
- Hip discomfort, sciatic-like pain down one leg, or a "catching" sensation in the groin
- Upper-back and neck tension from bigger breasts and changed posture
- Rib pain as the growing uterus pushes upward in the third trimester
- General muscular tightness, headaches, or a body that just feels "wound up" as it adapts

You don't need to be in pain to come in — many patients book a check-in each trimester purely as maintenance.

## What treatment actually looks like

Your first visit takes 45–60 minutes. We take a full history — how the pregnancy is going, what your midwife or usual doctor has said, any prior back or pelvic issues, how you're sleeping — and then do a gentle assessment of your posture, spine, pelvis and hips. Based on what we find, treatment might include:

- **Soft-tissue release** across the lower back, glutes, hip flexors and diaphragm
- **Gentle joint mobilisation** of the pelvis, lumbar spine, ribs and mid-back
- **Cranial and functional techniques** — very light-touch, often used for people who prefer minimal pressure or are managing high blood pressure or nausea
- **Breathing and pelvic-floor coordination work** — small changes to how you breathe and brace can meaningfully offload the low back
- **Practical advice** on sleep position, sitting, lifting a toddler, exercise and pelvic supports where useful

We work in side-lying or semi-reclined positions from about 20 weeks onward, propped with pillows or wedges. Nothing is forceful, nothing requires you to be flat on your back for long, and you can always ask to change position or stop.

## How this fits with your midwife or usual doctor

Osteopathy sits alongside your usual maternity care, not instead of it. Your midwife or LMC remains the person coordinating your pregnancy and birth; we focus on the musculoskeletal side — the aches, stiffness, and postural adaptations that are outside their scope but affect how well you sleep and function day-to-day. If we notice anything that needs their attention (persistent one-sided pain, unusual swelling, blood-pressure concerns), we'll tell you and encourage you to raise it with them.
